# Desert Base and City Crossroads mobile design playtest

## Test conditions

I played the English Standard / Regular prototypes from the Skirmish tile in the Mac Unity Editor using the visible player controls. ARIA displayed **Play** during manual play. Both original launches used seed 104729. All mission times below use the 18-minute match clock. On Desert Base the Editor lost foreground focus, so I repeated the test using a temporary Unity Input System touchscreen. I chose the on-screen taps myself. That path let me verify squad selection, command modes and the tactical map without depending on desktop focus. These are agent-operated designer sessions. Thumb reach, pinch accuracy, audio, haptics, device frame pacing and first-time human win rates still need a phone test. Owner sampling remains open.

## Desert Base · S002

This is the stronger brief: armor contests the highway, one squad guards home, and the player can push or scout. I would keep that tradeoff. On entering the match I saw runway markings and then scattered infantry. The dark tarmac gives soldiers a clearer silhouette than City's pale sand. The white stripes dominate the frame. Of the first five squad cards, three were rifle squads and none was the tank or APC named in the briefing. The camera was close enough to distinguish troops, too close to show my barracks, the highway approach and the first threat together. The tactical map finally gave the battlefield a shape, but I had to open the minimap to discover **Center on HQ**.

**Manual ending.** I saw my base drop from 800 to 632, then to 100. At **1:35** it fell. The defeat panel reported **zero units lost, zero enemies defeated and one building lost**. It marked **Keep squad losses low — Failed**. A player with an intact army receives no explanation for that failed objective. The large green star beside the red defeat title also sends mixed signals. The attacker was outside my view at the decisive moment. I read the declining numbers, but saw no shot or impact that told me where to respond.

The first attempt had unconfirmed mouse orders. On the same-seed touch replays I saw squad check marks, a 20-soldier selection and the Move / Attack targeting instructions. I still could not frame the shooter and intercept it before the base fell. On one replay the tactical map showed my base at 44/800. In the first run the enemy trace identifies one tank dealing direct damage at 67.22 seconds, then reaching 72/800 at 92.63. In another it began at 70.88 and reached 72/800 at 97.17. This confirms a roughly half-minute response window on this seed. It does not establish that interception is impossible. [Desert Base manual and touch replay log](/private/tmp/warline-designer-s002-20261117.log)

**Design judgment.** The ambush is worth keeping. I want to lose the base because I knowingly committed my reserve, then replay to make the other choice. The present opening let me lose with all 20 infantry still listed and no clear sight of the attack. Give the first hit a directional warning and a single tap to frame the barracks and its visible approach. Put the defenders named in the briefing within easy reach. Test that a player who heeds the warning can land a defensive order while the response window is still open. If the warning and order work and the reserve still cannot intervene, then tune the approach or starting placement.

I also observed a repeat defeat at **1:37**, again showing zero unit losses, zero enemies defeated and one building lost. From that panel I deliberately tapped **Main Menu** and verified both the restored campaign / Skirmish menu and the completed `route=MainMenu` transition in the Unity log. Retry also started another playable match.

## City Crossroads · S026

The win condition fits in one sentence and the HUD gives a usable instruction to select a squad, tap Attack, then tap the target. The missing information was geographic. The mission opened on a knot of camouflaged infantry, pale rocks and a bright palm. I could identify individual soldiers but could not see a crossroads, my barracks or a direction of advance. The setup used the same city image as Desert Base and left the space for a tactical briefing blank.

Tapping cards grew the selection from four to eight to twenty soldiers. The check marks, total health and **Advancing and engaging** status rewarded these taps. Tapping **Enemy Base** put a small amber label over bare rock while the barracks was outside vision. When my soldiers reached it, a roof filled much of the view and the enemy health changed from a dash to 800/800. The distinction between an attack on the approach and an attack on the building became clear only in hindsight. Camera focus and issuing an order should remain independent.

My infantry push got the barracks down to 624/800. I bought a battle tank and saw the target fall to 324/800. I lost the opening infantry and the tank and recruited rocketeers and breachers to try again. The mid-match recovery was the most interesting decision. The recruitment artwork and action button were clear. Returning from recruitment sometimes framed the underside of a large aircraft, while selecting troops and finding their fight required more taps. Cards also changed positions as squads died. The air-attack countdown created a good moment of urgency. I could read the alert more readily than I could connect a combat effect to a particular loss.

Shortly after an enemy-order trace at 9:36 elapsed, Unity logged a 16 MiB Entities BlockAllocator exception, followed by command-buffer, invalid-entity and null-reference errors. Later the clock continued while repeated, focused taps on squad cards and on **Enemy Base** produced no visible selection or camera feedback. These observations coincided. The log does not establish their causal chain. This run cannot support raising enemy strength. The earlier automated victory at 2:32 shows what a practiced input policy can do on a different seed.

The City Crossroads configuration explicitly reuses the Desert Base source, as the Campaign map policy allows. The name and artwork create an expectation of an intersection. A better opening anchor, yaw and preview from the existing location can fulfill that promise. [City Crossroads source binding](../../../Assets/Game/Configs/Skirmish/CityCrossroads/OperationMap_CityCrossroads.asset)

**Manual ending.** I stayed until the full 18-minute deadline. Immediately before expiry my barracks still showed 800/800. At expiry the base counters vanished, the resource strip switched to **Civilian Risk MED**, and the bottom row became generic Rifle Squad / Vehicles / Helicopter / Jet / Transport cards. A result panel, retry and normal return never appeared during the subsequent checks. The Editor log also recorded a failure in `SkirmishResultSettlementSystem.OnUpdate` at the transition. I ended Play mode and restarted the Editor before testing Desert Base. Record this as **deadline reached, settlement failed**. The two-star 2:32 win pictured below is the separate ARIA reference.

## Work for the next candidates

The main menu, commander portraits, unit illustrations and colored command buttons make a stronger first impression than either battlefield opening. The game already has the art and troops to compose a better shot. I would spend the next visual pass on what the camera shows together and on how combat reads in motion. Adding scenery to empty corners would leave both problems in place. In City, the resource choice between a tank and replacement infantry was engaging. In Desert, the reserve-versus-attack choice was promising on the briefing screen but hard to experience during play.

## Planning the next mission family

Opus owns the player decision for each prototype. The runtime and UI owners handle a shared defect once for all missions. Put one line in each existing brief covering what the player sees, the first order, the mid-match choice and the payoff. Use the existing offline checks for data and placement. In Unity, sample both a quick competent finish and a slower recovery from a bad opening. Keep one long-session regression for the shared runtime. Use a fresh player on a small phone for the first example of each objective family, then reserve additional device sessions for meaningful differences in camera, controls or rules.

Keep Frontline Control, Breakthrough and Convoy Escort authoring moving alongside these shared fixes. One recognizable representative of each reveals more about the remaining catalog than polishing two Base Assault timings in isolation. Reuse the resulting controls and evidence workflow for Operations after validating the Skirmish families.
