## Chapter Promise

Qassem's hidden network has been exposed, so the Vanguard Brigade escalates openly with aircraft, armored columns, air defense, and long-range fire. The Commander must use WarlineCapture's full combined-arms toolset without accepting Qassem's argument that greater threat justifies unrestricted force.

```text
The battlefield becomes larger and heavier.
The standard for a legitimate target does not become lower.
```

## Conventional-War Lane

Vanguard Brigade establishes that WarlineCapture can support military-versus-military conflict as well as counter-insurgent operations.

- Vanguard personnel use distinct regular/proxy military presentation, not insurgent civilian-area disguise.
- Aircraft, armor, and launchers have organized doctrine, clear approach lanes, and readable warnings.
- The Brigade is fictional and not identified with a real neighboring state.
- Ash Line responsibility for terrorism remains explicit; conventional escalation does not retroactively make every opponent a terrorist.
- Future campaigns may introduce rival states, coalition forces, ceasefires, naval operations, and peacekeeping through this lane.

## Feature Contract

|---|---|---|
| Radar and automatic G2A | Protect relief corridors through coverage, warning, and launcher placement. | Detection, range, engagement, ammo/readiness, warning, accessibility, and protection must be clear. |

| Parachute personnel drop | Trade precision and safety for rapid insertion. | Valid drop zones, scatter/survival, ownership, and landing readability must be validated. |

| Fuel network | Limit heavy force through preparation rather than arbitrary cooldown. | Required mission usage cannot begin in an unwinnable Fuel state. |
| Tactical follow cinematic | Emphasize selected air or long-range hero moments. | Never hide urgent warnings, command state, or civilian risk. |

## Reward And Progression Direction

- Early: Fuel, armor protection, radar/G2A capability, and air-readiness progression.
- Middle: fixed G2G or support capability only at a point where range and risk UX are ready.
- Late: aircraft, cargo, or transport progression tied to mastered insertion behavior.
- Completion: Protocol Fragment 4, combined-arms chapter recognition, and Chapter 5 access.

Do not introduce unrelated naval rewards in a land/air chapter unless a coastal operation is actually authored and validated. Naval units are better reserved for Skirmish, Operations, or a future coastal campaign.

## Presentation Direction

- Chapter opening must clearly change the silhouette of the war: columns, aircraft, organized radio traffic, and military staging replace hidden-cell ambiguity.
- M01 warnings use audio, direction, time-to-impact, and non-color state.
- M03 depicts G2G preparation and target verification; it must not present long-range fire as a casual button.
- M04 contrasts runway unloading, parachute insertion, and cargo drop through readable operational choices.
- M05 uses a limited hero cinematic and a Tier B reveal of the two final keys.

## Balance Direction

Use Standard bands early and Mastery bands for M03-M05.

- Air and armor threats need fair warning and visible counterplay.
- Fuel creates planning pressure but cannot hard-lock required tutorial actions.
- Automatic G2A must reward placement and protection without requiring manual target tapping.
- G2G minimum range and preparation create tactical exposure, not opaque delay.
- Airborne insertion must be risky because of known conditions, not random unit loss.

## High-Level Validation Questions

- Are Vanguard and Ash Line roles visually and narratively distinct?
- Can the player understand air/armor warning direction and response time?
- Does every G2G action expose range, preparation, confirmation, and civilian risk before commitment?
- Do transport, parachute, and cargo states remain readable on mobile?
- Does ARIA reject unrestricted control consistently in story and gameplay?
- Is Protocol Fragment 4 guaranteed by completion?
- Does the chapter expand future military-war possibilities without referencing a real current state or conflict?
