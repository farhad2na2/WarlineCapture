# Command Siege (S002) · designer retest, 18 November

**Keep the mission and the opening ambush. Hold player release.** The strongest moment was turning four Rocketeers toward the threat, watching the HQ stop losing health at 464/800, and deciding how many units I could afford to send away. A new player can easily miss that moment. The opening shows parked aircraft, the camera sends the first fight to the edge of the screen, and the next objective looks like a status readout.

## What I played

I used the normal Skirmish library, Command Siege, Standard, Regular, English, seed 104729, with ARIA off. Opus reserved the Mac Editor for S002. Checkout was `missions/integration` at `e2957bc7b`, with documentation updates after the requested candidate. The 1920×1080 Game view received ordinary touch input through a temporary Unity Input System touchscreen. The initial touchscreen calibration missed some controls, so I excluded that attempt from control judgments and repeated them. Times are match time. This was a designer test in the Editor. Thumb targeting, pinch, haptics, audio and first-time human success still need a device session.

## Fixes in priority order

| Priority and owner | Player experience and requested change | Acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| P1 · Opus / level design | The airfield has a recognizable silhouette, but a parked fighter and a hangar roof dominate the opening. None of the first five cards controls a jet. Frame the existing HQ, its approach and a playable squad together. Preserve the tank's timing for the next test. | Freeze the first playable frame on a phone. A fresh player can point to what they are defending, where trouble could come from and one unit they can order. |
| P1 · HUD / camera / VFX | The orange warning is easy to notice and the Rocketeer card gets a useful outline. The alert tap frames home. In the resulting view the red arrow points across bare sand toward the extreme right. I could not readily distinguish the attacker, its shot and the impact. The two warning rows shift vertically when the top banner expires. | Keep the action in a stable location. While the attacker is visible, frame both it and the HQ inside the unobstructed play area and show one readable shot or impact. Label the defender control for the action it performs. Camera focus must leave unit orders to the player. |
| P1 · Opus / mission guidance | The library gives a clear goal. In the match, “Enemy Base · Not in Sight” looks passive. Tapping it actually frames a valuable reported position. The map legend and road grid gave me little help choosing where to scout. | After a successful defense, surface the existing reported-position action and a short choice, for example “Keep a guard at HQ. Scout the reported barracks.” A fresh player can send a scout and identify the reserve without coaching. Preserve fog of war. |
| P2 · Result / UX | The red defeat emblem and “Met · needs a win” finally explain zero losses. Both early and late defeats say the base was lost. | Preserve the star explanation. If the attacker was known, add one recap line identifying what destroyed the HQ. Use the library mission name on the result so a player can recognize the retry. |

Squad cards and their selection ticks worked for assembling mixed groups. The yawed ground view and north-up map made the first turn hard to translate. The revised zoom shows more terrain, but blank sand around a largely off-screen fight gives the player less information than a closer view of the right place. For the next Regular test, observe a fresh phone player through the first defense and a counterattack or a clearly understood loss. If that player sees the tank, gives a timely order and still routinely loses to it, revisit defender starting placement or the response window. ## Helicopter camera

I reviewed Opus's S026 captures separately. The turn frame shows the helicopter's shadow more clearly than the airframe. The logged camera position stays the same to two decimal places across the takeoff, turn and switch-back sequence. The selected card reads Transport Helicopter on the switch-back screenshot. These captures leave Farhad's flight-follow concern open. Capture a short player-directed flight on a phone, including a turn, landing and a switch to infantry. Review whether the player can read the whole rotor, heading and intended landing patch together and reacquire the ground squad. S026 was outside this live reservation.
