The r4 [`NNN-urp-owners.json` snapshots](/private/tmp/s005-native1464r4-resource-census/000-urp-owners.json) identify **exactly 19** of each Replay's 76 retained objects in `VolumeManager.m_ComponentsDefaultState` at that Fresh boundary. Every such 19-object set remains a live native reference at the final Menu even after later boundaries replace the manager's current array. The other **57 per Replay** are absent from the owner fields captured at these settled boundaries: current/default/created VolumeStacks and `UniversalAdditionalCameraData.s_CachedVolumeStacks` have no stack entries in the snapshots. This observer does not capture every managed reference or intermediate pipeline-switch frame, so it cannot identify those 57 as stack objects, an active engine cache, or unreferenced objects. In the installed URP Core source, `VolumeManager.InitializeInternal` builds default state and a stack, while `EvaluateVolumeDefaultState` constructs a new ScriptableObject per type and replaces `m_ComponentsDefaultState` without destroying the previous array; `Deinitialize` disposes stacks but has no corresponding default-state destruction (`Library/PackageCache/com.unity.render-pipelines.core@9fe9377312a8/Runtime/Volume/VolumeManager.cs:285`, `:303`, `:526`, `:584`). This explains the positively identified old default-state survivors and makes additional default-state replacements during pipeline switches a strong **inference**, not a proven owner for the remaining 57.

The shared Campaign lighting owner creates a new `Campaign Map Rendering` URP asset, assigns `QualitySettings.renderPipeline`, and restores the previous asset on destruction (`Assets/Game/Scripts/Composition/SkirmishS004CampaignLighting.cs:95`, `:112`, `:115`). The 11 Fresh r4 boundaries show 11 different nonpersistent `Campaign Map Rendering` asset IDs; **none of those clone IDs** survives the final Menu. Thus the mission's repeated rendering-profile switch triggers the URP allocations, while the retained components belong to the rendering subsystem rather than a scene actor. Their growth is repeatable and unbounded over this ten-Replay sample, so it is not established as a benign bounded cache or as a 7 GB mission leak. The r4 equivalent-Fresh native-allocated counter rises about 7.38 GB, but process resident memory falls about 1.91 GB and graphics-driver bytes fall about 224 MB ([r4 memory diagnostic](native1464r4-memory-diagnostic.json)); those global Editor/census counters cannot be attributed to the ~0.42 MiB URP cohort.
